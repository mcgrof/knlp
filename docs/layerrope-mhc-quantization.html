The hypothesis worth testing

The hypothesis is that LayerRoPE’s depth-dependent gains could improve tolerance to weight quantization, either alone or combined with mHC. This study tests that possibility; it is not a result established by the LayerRoPE paper.

One possible mechanism is that a depth-conditioned gain schedule changes weight ranges or redistributes sensitivity to rounding. Multiple residual streams and constrained routing might also distribute perturbations. They might instead increase storage or move sensitivity elsewhere. Neither intuition establishes a gain; the test must compare clean loss, damaged loss, bytes, and compute.

Training
Clean CE

Lower cross-entropy at equal tokens, plus elapsed time. A lower final loss alone does not establish faster convergence.

Quantization
ΔCE

Quantized CE minus that checkpoint’s own clean CE, on identical final inputs. Also report absolute quantized CE.

Compression
Bytes

Count packed weights, scale storage, and unquantized parameters. A tolerant submodule is not automatically a smaller whole model.

Weight quantization only. These are post-training weight-rounding screens, evaluated after dequantization. They do not test KV-cache quantization, integer activations, quantization-aware training, packed inference kernels, latency, or real packed-memory allocation.

The mechanisms are composable. Their gains need not add. A depth code changes the update rule at a layer; it does not attach an immutable layer identity to each contribution after streams have been mixed.

The write code and carry code, precisely

For four streams, use a balanced phase code:

c(φ) = [cos φ, sin φ, −cos φ, −sin φ]
s = minᵢ(βᵢ, 2 − βᵢ)
β′ = β + 0.5 s c(φ(ℓ))

Here β is the original vector of write coefficients in [0, 2]. The bounded correction preserves that range. M-O (V01) uses the ordered layer schedule. V02 applies a fixed shuffled layer lookup. V03 uses the middle-depth phase everywhere. The schedule has four learned scalars across the attention and MLP paths.

V30 instead adds 0.5 × c cᵀ / 2 to the carry logits before Sinkhorn normalization; its write coefficients stay unchanged. V30-S shuffles the phase lookup before forming the outer product. V30-C uses the middle-depth phase. The outer product has rank one and is invariant to a π phase shift, so its depth code has different symmetries from the write code.

The Birkhoff-geometry interpretation of mHC (Audio Summary) can help reason about mean and difference modes of an exactly doubly stochastic carry map. It does not make finite-iteration routing exact, guarantee quantization robustness, or turn a mixed residual stream into a record of each layer’s contribution.

The first depth screen supplied a lead, not a winner

The initial TinyStories experiment used the paper’s LLaMA backbone at width 128, two heads, FFN width 352, and depths 4, 16, and 32. Three paired seeds ran for 2,000 steps (4.096 million predicted tokens). The study included 64 learning-rate pilots and 48 final trajectories, on an RTX 4090. The five families were residual (R), LayerRoPE (L), mHC (M), full combination (ML), and depth-code-only mHC (M-O; tested at depth 32).

Discovery and confirmation were kept separate

A 32-variant screen varied code amplitude, placement, phase, basis, read/write/carry insertion, and learned frequency. Stage A screened at 1,000 steps; Stage B continued the selected rows to 2,000 steps, using three discovery seeds on a W7900.

V20, a learned-frequency write variant, was the raw Stage B leader for training and quantization: primary CE 2.610010 and INT4 CE 2.528025, versus 2.617378 and 2.551336 for mHC. An unresolved historical numerical miss prevented qualification. It was not silently replaced in the discovery ranking, and it was not independently confirmed.

The frozen confirmation nominees were V30 for training and V02 for quantization. Their ordered, shuffled, and constant controls formed seven rows. Stage C used eight fresh paired seeds, 4,000 steps, and reserved document-disjoint evaluation arrays. All 56 trajectories completed on the frozen H100 runtime. Stage D, the proposed deeper-model test, stayed disabled because no nominee passed.

Frozen confirmation recipe and acceptance rules

Depth 32, width 128, sequence length 256, 2,048 predicted tokens per update, 8.192 million tokens per run; seeds 76411–76418. Learning rate 0.003 for every row, AdamW (0.9, 0.95), epsilon 10−8, zero weight decay, no clipping, 100-step warm-up then constant rate. BF16 activations with FP32 routing; 20 Sinkhorn iterations, epsilon 10−6. The frozen H100 runtime used torch 2.9.1+cu128 with TF32 and compilation disabled.

Primary training CE is the mean at steps 2000, 2100, …, 4000 on the 32,768-token curve array. Final clean and quantized CE use the separate 131,072-token final array. Quantization damage uses the CPU clean endpoint from the same checkpoint; it must not be calculated by subtracting the GPU curve metric.

  • Training: win all eight primary pairs, improve mean CE by at least 0.005, improve mean final CE, and have no final seed regression above 0.01.
  • Quantization: improve absolute INT4 CE in all eight seeds; reduce mean damage by at least 0.01 versus mHC or 0.005 versus M-O; reduce damage in at least seven seeds; no clean endpoint regret above 0.01.
  • Ordered carry mechanism: beat both shuffled and constant carry in all eight primary pairs, by at least 0.005 on average.
  • Retain the separate numerical qualification. Performance alone cannot waive a numerical miss.

How far the negative finding goes: all six predeclared claims failed their performance and qualification rules. This rejects promotion under those rules; it does not prove equivalence, rule out a small average benefit, or disprove full LayerRoPE. Discovery and confirmation changed hardware, seeds, evaluation data, and duration, so their difference cannot be assigned to one factor.

Coverage mattered more than the tested depth codes

All 56 checkpoints passed both block-only INT4 and block-only INT3 under the +0.1-nat damage limit. None passed all-matrix INT4, all-matrix INT3, or group-128 INT4. This pattern holds across every confirmed architecture row.

The block-only path quantizes about 31.925% of parameters. It leaves both the token embedding and the language-model output head unquantized. The all-matrix path covers about 95.878%. This localizes the large observed penalty to the broader coverage change; it does not identify which excluded matrix dominates, or establish additive error attribution.

Quantizer definition and coverage

The primary screen uses symmetric per-row max-absolute scaling with nearest rounding, without calibration or retraining. Each case starts again from the same clean checkpoint. All eligible matrices means the backbone embedding and linear weights; normalization gains and routing/code controls are not integer-quantized.

qmax = 2b−1 − 1
scale = max(|w|) / qmax
ŵ = scale × clamp(round(w / scale), −qmax, qmax)

Zero-scale rows are handled without division by zero. Group-128 repeats the max-absolute calculation within groups of 128 columns, padding the last group as needed. Scales are FP32. The block-only case selects backbone layer linears, excluding both the embedding and output head. The weights are dequantized for CPU model evaluation. INT2 in the screen has three symmetric levels; it is not a four-level asymmetric codebook.

The quality threshold is a per-checkpoint CE increase no greater than 0.1 nat, equivalent to a perplexity ratio no greater than exp(0.1), about 1.105. This is the frozen engineering tolerance, not a proof that the change is perceptually negligible.

They also do not establish that LayerRoPE is uniquely tolerant: the experiment concerns these depth-code variants, and small routing-rounding effects appear in plain mHC too.

Numerical qualification remained a separate gate

V01, V02, V03, and V30 retained numerical misses in the prescribed historical/confirmation scope. V00, V30-S, and V30-C passed that scope. A successful checkpoint resume or a good final loss cannot clear an earlier tolerance failure.

Some discrepancies were small in absolute terms. One retained V01 comparison missed by about 8.27×10−9 against a 5.46×10−6 reference, giving relative error 0.001514 above the 0.001 threshold. Agreement in FP64 for the same finite function points to sensitivity between FP32 evaluation graphs; it does not justify changing the frozen tolerance after seeing results.

Keep two questions separate: whether the implemented 20-step Sinkhorn function and its derivative agree with a same-function oracle, and whether that finite map approximates an ideal converged projection. Replacing the operator with a much longer solve changes the experiment.

Useful findings without an architecture winner

TRAINING AND DEPTH

No tested candidate earned a robust convergence or quantization claim in confirmation. No depth-scaling claim follows because Stage D did not run.

WEIGHT PRECISION

Coverage controls explain a large practical difference in quantization tolerance. BF16 storage of routing controls has small measured loss effects; four-scalar storage savings are negligible.

OPEN QUESTION

Does full LayerRoPE change embedding/head sensitivity relative to block weights at a fixed byte budget? The depth-code confirmation does not answer this question.

The next useful comparison would separate embedding and output-head quantization, then compare full LayerRoPE with matched baselines at the same storage budget. The current results support no general claim that adding a depth signal improves quantization or convergence.

Reproduce the training and quantization experiments

The aggregate route downloads pinned public TinyStories data, runs the initial depth screen, then executes the fixed seven-row confirmation on two H100 GPUs. Operator and exact-resume checks precede measured training. Clean and quantized endpoint losses use the same held-out inputs. Use make defconfig-layerrope-mhc-tinystories-smoke followed by make for a bounded CPU check with synthetic tokens.

make defconfig-layerrope-mhc-tinystories
make

Install the dependencies in the reproduction guide. Run a separately named smoke preset first to check your environment. Configure data, output, checkpoints, device and time limits with make menuconfig; each run saves its effective settings and reports incomplete work.

The confirmation recipe uses seven rows, eight paired seeds, depth 32, width 128, and 4,000 training steps. Keep full LayerRoPE, the write-code family, and the carry-code family distinct. The initial depth screen and the confirmation use different evaluation partitions and must produce separate reports.

The run produces per-seed clean losses, quantization damage, absolute quantized losses, parameter coverage, ideal storage bytes, timings, and numerical-check outcomes. Each quantization case restarts from the same clean checkpoint. Smoke output is labeled separately and cannot issue a scientific confirmation verdict.

To extend the experiment, change the configuration while retaining paired baselines and fixed evaluation inputs. Useful tests include embedding-only versus head-only quantization, full LayerRoPE at a matched byte budget, and deeper models after a candidate shows a repeatable advantage. Keep new hypotheses separate from the original confirmation.
