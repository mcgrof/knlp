1. What a cartridge is, and what CAS adds

A cartridge is a trained KV cache. Train it once per document offline, inject it into the model's KV cache at serving time, and skip prefill. The base technique trains that prefix by self-study context distillation: a teacher answers synthetic questions about the document, and the cartridge is trained to match the teacher's next-token distribution. One document, one cartridge, one prefix at serving time.

Cartridges at Scale keeps that per-document cartridge and adds four things:

  • Scale to a collection. One cartridge per document, many resident on the GPU at once, any subset composed at request time.
  • A budget manager. During training, keep a fixed number of cartridges on the GPU and rotate the active set every few steps so a large collection trains within memory.
  • Mixed-visibility joint training. Independently trained cartridges interfere when placed in one cache. CAS co-trains them with a per-sample visibility rule so they learn to coexist.
  • Collapse and rescue as the headline: isolated cartridges collapse toward the no-context floor when co-loaded; the joint rule prevents it.

The reproduction target is that story — split a collection into per-document cartridges, observe the collapse when they are combined, and recover it with the training-rule change — measured on Qwen3-8B and LongHealth.

3. The faithful isolated recipe

The optimizer regime was the missing lever in the earlier 0.50–0.55 plateau. Holding the patient and data fixed, moving from the small-batch recipe to the paper's global-batch-128 schedule raised patient 02 from 0.55 to 0.65 ± 0.05. Discard the earlier claims that learning rate and training length had been ruled out.

The schedule horizon is not the number of optimizer updates. For patient 02, 80 epochs end at roughly 1,000 updates while the learning rate is still about 0.083. The harness now stops explicitly at a requested step; the upstream trainer's max_optimizer_steps hook saves a checkpoint but does not otherwise stop training.

What the sink is. Position 0 is the frozen KV entry for the first system-template token, Qwen's <|im_start|>. Queries can place otherwise unhelpful attention mass on this stable slot instead of document-bearing slots. The model does attend to it; dropping it produces degenerate results.

5. The remaining quality gap

The main residual is generation control under the paper's 2,048-token cap, not simply missing document facts. Patients 03 and 05 hit the cap on about 28% of generations. Their completed answers score 0.81–0.86, above the paper's aggregate, while truncated answers fall near chance because the scorer sees the tail of unfinished reasoning. Patient 06 shows a smaller version of the same effect.

Observed behaviorInterpretation
Patients 03 and 05: 28% capped; completed accuracy 0.81–0.86knowledge is present; answer control is too long
Patient 06: 13% capped; completed accuracy 0.67mixed length and knowledge effect
Patient 01: median output 77 characters; completed accuracy 0.51genuine knowledge/representation gap

A 4,096-token cap ablation would quantify the length effect directly; it has not been run. The paper synthesizes roughly 40,000 conversations per cartridge, versus about 4,400 here, so data scale remains a plausible lever for patient 01. Earlier small-batch synthesis variants did not close the old 0.4–0.5 plateau, but they do not rule out better data under the faithful optimizer regime.

6. A100 cross-hardware control

A separately trained patient-02 cartridge on one A100 PCIe 40 GB, using the same faithful recipe, scored 0.833 ± 0.058: 0.80, 0.80, and 0.90, or 50 correct answers out of 60. The H100-trained cartridge scored 0.65, or 39 out of 60. The A100 run finished at optimizer step 1,068 with final training loss about 0.0153.

Do not call this an A100 advantage. The two runs produced different learned cartridges and evaluation is sampled. A matched cross-evaluation of both cartridge files with controlled generation seeds is required before attributing the difference to hardware or training trajectory. Today this is a surprising cross-hardware observation, not a causal result.

7. The cartridge path is lossless

The cartridge evaluator uses a custom FlexAttention Qwen3 forward, so the ordinary full-document anchor alone cannot validate it. The direct control prefills the complete roughly 12,000-token record, captures every exact K and V tensor, writes that full KV into cartridge format unchanged, and evaluates it through the cartridge path. It scores 0.86, matching ordinary full-document inference at 0.855.

The path has no measurable loss. This rules out positional offsets, RoPE handling, serialization, reconstruction, and cache loading as explanations for the learned-cartridge gap. It does not say that a 632-position learned cartridge is lossless; it says the execution path can carry the full document KV without loss. For patient 02, the full document is 12,628 tokens. Do not confuse this control with the 631 document-derived trainable positions used to initialize its learned cartridge.

8. Collapse/rescue status

The CAS paper reports that independently trained cartridges lose accuracy when co-loaded and that mixed-visibility joint training prevents the collapse. Treat the following measurements from the earlier page as invalid historical output:

Invalid historical armAloneCo-loadedDelta
Isolated cartridges0.580.38−0.20
Mixed-visibility joint0.440.46+0.02
Do not use those numbers. Their self-study data came through a vLLM teacher path that serialized the sampled token and then repeated it in the top-k list. Once real token IDs were added to an otherwise incomplete upstream path, greedy outputs turned that latent layout trap into live duplicate supervision. A separate earlier smoke was worse: every stored target ID was -1 and could not train the intended objective.

The affected synthesis parquet was not archived, so the classification rests on the generator code and run provenance rather than a row-by-row audit of that exact file. That uncertainty does not rescue the result: a run with unverified, known-affected targets cannot support the central claim.

Current status: keep the isolated reproduction in the clean result set. Mark collapse and rescue as open. Before citing a numerical result, run fresh deduplicated synthesis, evaluate isolated cartridges under co-load, and then train and evaluate the joint cartridges on those clean targets. The public harness already implements the paper's mixed-visibility rule with per-sample cache assembly.

9. Closed ablations and training stability

Meta-learned initialization: no useful speedup

A displacement fitted from other documents lowers the step-zero held-out loss, but training forgets it. The best step-60 affine initialization reached the baseline's 50%, 75%, and 90% loss-drop targets only 3.3, 5.0, and 5.6 steps earlier out of roughly 1,000 total steps. Remove its lower step-zero loss and it is slower. By step 180, baseline and fitted starts are within 0.00003. A same-seed replicate was bit-for-bit identical through step 110, so “independent replicate” here means a determinism control, not new statistical evidence. This line is closed.

Reverse KL: no measurable gain

A reverse-KL warm-start pilot moved patient 02 from 0.65 ± 0.05 to 0.70 ± 0.05 after 200 steps: 42 versus 39 correct answers out of 60, less than one binomial standard deviation. It did not collapse, but this evaluation does not resolve a gain.

The paper learning rate can spike

The 0.1 peak learning rate uses plain Adam without gradient clipping. Three of five anchor-initialized H100 runs showed a loss spike after warmup; all recovered within about 60 steps. Whether clipping improves final accuracy is untested. This is a stability caveat, not evidence against the recipe that produced the quality gain.

10. Code and reproduction

The public research/cartridges_cas harness contains the synthesis, isolated and joint trainers, Table-15 evaluator, cache-path parity control, prompt-grouped validation split, checkpoint controls, and meta-initialization analysis.

Three rules are load-bearing:

  • Validate generated targets. Token IDs in every stored row must be real, unique within the top-k support, and checked at write time.
  • Reconstruct the right cache. An isolated checkpoint needs its one frozen sink plus trainable KV. A joint checkpoint's frozen region contains distractors, so an oracle read loads only the target's trainable region plus its required sink representation.
  • Validate the protocol first. No-context must land near 0.39 and full-document inference near 0.855 before a cartridge score is interpretable. A single 20-question run is too noisy; use at least three.

Compiled FlexAttention on CUDA sm≥80 gives about a 16× training speedup over the raw path. The target flattener also preserves the full available top-k when cumulative probability does not reach its threshold, instead of silently falling back to top-1.

11. Serving

The serving side is already built. A cartridge injects as a KVConnectorBase_V1 plugin into vLLM with no core modifications, and on this record the time-to-first-token drops from 757 ms (re-prefilling the ~12K-token document every query) to 81 ms (loading the 611-token KV prefix), a 677 ms median saving per query at equal decode throughput. The connector, the multi-cartridge router, GPU residency and eviction, and an asymmetric-KV storage codec have all been implemented and tested against vLLM and LMCache.

CAS makes that plumbing broadly useful: a document collection can be compiled once, kept resident under a budget, and selected per request without repeatedly prefilling each retrieved document. Quantization of the stored cartridge is a separate serving R&D result and is intentionally documented separately from this training reproduction.
